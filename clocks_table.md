# Biological Aging Clocks - Summary Table

Source: Wyss-Coray, T. & Topol, E. J. "Biological aging clocks in health and disease."
*Nature Medicine* 32, 2383–2394 (2026). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04495-3

This table catalogs every named aging clock mentioned in the review, pulled from
the main text and reference list, along with the original paper and any public
code/model implementation we've located. **Vendored Implementation** points at
this repo's own copy, once a clock's code has been vendored in (see
[`clocks/`](clocks/)); **Other Implementations** lists public code we found
elsewhere but haven't vendored (yet, or at all).

## Notes

- **AdaptAge** - identity confirmed via PMID [38243142](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38243142/): Ying, K. et al. "Causality-enriched epigenetic age uncouples damage and adaptation." *Nat. Aging* 4, 231–246 (2024) - this is ref. 46 in the review's own bibliography, matched exactly. The paper introduces a *pair* of clocks, DamAge and AdaptAge, built via epigenome-wide Mendelian randomization + elastic net to separate CpGs with causal detrimental vs. adaptive effects on aging-related traits - not a deep learning or transformer model, and not trained on chronological age.
  **Root cause of the mismatch, found in the review's own body text** (page 3, discussing Table 1): *"the fourth generation of DNA methylation clocks introduces deep learning transformer models to gain insights about causality, such as with AdaptAge, DamAge and Skip-Improved Training Hive (SITH) models, the latter arising from an open competition for biomarkers of aging<sup>46,47</sup>."* The review cites refs. 46 (Ying et al. 2024 - AdaptAge/DamAge, NOT deep learning) and 47 (Ying et al. 2026, the open competition paper - SITH and other genuine deep-learning entrants) together in one sentence, and appears to lump AdaptAge/DamAge in with SITH under a single "deep learning transformer models" description. **Conclusion: this looks like an inaccuracy/oversimplification in the review's own text/Table 1**, not a wrong citation on our part - AdaptAge and DamAge should be classified as causal-inference (Mendelian randomization + elastic net) clocks, generation 3rd/4th-adjacent but not deep learning, while only SITH (and possibly other ref. 47 competition entrants) are true deep-learning 4th-gen models.
- **GrimAge / GrimAge2**: original coefficients are not publicly released by the authors (licensing/commercial restriction via the Clock Foundation); `biolearn`'s implementation exists with the original authors' permission, not from a public release - flag this if licensing clarity is needed downstream.
- **Commercial-only clocks** (no public implementation, proprietary tests): GrimAge/GrimAge2 (partially), iAge (Edifice Health), Dementia SomaSignal Test (SomaLogic).
- Several clocks in the "Other" section have **no located public implementation** despite reasonably thorough search (MileAge, sperm sncRNA clock, IMM-AGE, retinal eyeAge, MRI+EEG brain clock, cell clocks, SITH) - these may require contacting authors directly, or reimplementing from the paper's methods/supplement.
- Research for this table was done via web search (Aug 2026); links should be spot-checked before being relied on for actual implementation work, since automated fetches were blocked for some paywalled Nature/Science pages and citation details for those were cross-verified via PubMed/PMC/secondary sources instead.
